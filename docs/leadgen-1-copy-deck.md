# Lead gen test 1: copy deck (v4, Copy School standard)

Written 2026-10-12 to the Copy School rules in Vantage. Opener per Sam's ruling: a value-prop hero in the buyer's words that names the pain, our proof directly under it, the pain held one beat later. No "I'm Sam" opener. Buyer language from the bank is in quotation marks in this deck so it stays theirs.

Page plan header. Persona: founder or head of growth at a dev-tool or cloud-infra vendor, past first customers, $20K+ deals, technical buyer, no sales hire. Decision makers: competitive (top of page: numbers, named pieces) and methodical (bottom: the How, the Switch, the FAQ). Awareness: solution aware, most of them burned once. What they want to do: find out if this is real before paying. Offer: $99 pilot deposit, credited to the first meeting; the free 3-prospect sample is the proof mechanism. Metric: sample requests and $99 payments.

### FAQ (Ry's order)

Crosshead
> What founders ask before they start

1. **How much of my time does this take?**
   About an hour to write the qualification bar with me. After that, the only time you spend is in the meetings themselves. A 20-minute meeting with a $20,000 buyer is the best hour you'll have that week.
2. **What if the meetings are bad?**
   You only pay for meetings that match the bar we wrote together. One that doesn't is credited. One that no-shows is rescheduled and not billed. You can't be billed for a bad meeting.
3. **Don't I need volume?**
   If your deals are $20,000 and up, 3 right meetings a month beat 30 wrong ones. The agencies that promise 10 to 60 a month staff them with reps who don't know your product. That's the thing their own customers complain about most.
4. **Why not an agency, or a hire?**
   An agency charges a retainer before a meeting exists and puts a rep on your account who has never used your product. A hire costs a salary and 3 months of ramp. The pilot costs $99 to find out, and you pay the rest only when a meeting happens.
5. **Why does it matter that an engineer does the outreach?**
   Because your buyers are engineers. When one asks what your tool does to their deploy pipeline, a rep says "great question, let me loop in the team." An engineer answers it. That answer is the difference between a reply and silence.
6. **What if it doesn't work for my product?**
   Then you'll know by day 2, when the first 10 prospects land. And if 3 meetings don't happen in 30 days, the $99 comes back.
7. **What exactly do I pay?**
   $99 today, credited to your first meeting. Then $600 per meeting that happens. Nothing else. No retainer, no setup fee, nothing auto-renews.
8. **What's the guarantee?**
   3 meetings in 30 days or your $99 back. A 14-day refund on the $99 for any reason. Reply to your Stripe receipt with "refund" and it's done.

## Sweeps run on this deck
- Clarity: every crosshead passes "X is Y" or states an outcome. No shorthand.
- Voice: plain, Sam's register, contractions, numerals.
- So what: every How block ends in a so-that; every proof column says what it proves.
- Prove it: the only first-party proof is the operator's background and the free demonstration; no invented stats, no testimonials claimed.
- Specificity: 48 hours, 10 prospects, $600, $0, 20 minutes, 3 in 30, day 2 vs month 3.
- Emotion: carried by the mined quotes and the Switch table, not adjectives.
- Zero risk: call-to-value buttons, a trigger under every button, the refund under the button, the anxiety line on the form.
- Tells: no em dashes, no "not X but Y", no "real" as intensifier, no greetings, no bow lines, no "just following up".
